# Pharmacology and toxicology
Toxicology|Tylenol overdose|ACETAMINOPHEN|RUMACKMATTHEW ACETYLCYSTEINE GLUTATHIONE NAPQI HEPATOTOXICITY FOURHOUR TRANSAMINASES NOMOGRAM|Start N-acetylcysteine for an acetaminophen level above the treatment line on the Rumack-Matthew nomogram, ideally within 8 hours.
Toxicology|Aspirin overdose|SALICYLATE|TINNITUS ALKALINIZATION BICARBONATE DIALYSIS RESPIRATORY ALKALOSIS HYPERTHERMIA|Salicylate toxicity: mixed respiratory alkalosis and anion gap acidosis; alkalinize the urine and dialyze severe cases.
Toxicology|Depressants|BENZODIAZEPINE|FLUMAZENIL LORAZEPAM DIAZEPAM ALPRAZOLAM MIDAZOLAM WITHDRAWAL SEDATION|Avoid flumazenil in chronic benzodiazepine users or unknown overdoses; it can provoke seizures.
Toxicology|Wide QRS overdose|TRICYCLICS|AMITRIPTYLINE BICARBONATE QRS WIDENING SEIZURES ANTICHOLINERGIC TERMINAL SODIUM|Tricyclic overdose with a QRS above 100 ms or a terminal R in aVR: give IV sodium bicarbonate.
Toxicology|Heart pills overdose|DIGOXIN|HYPERKALEMIA YELLOWVISION BIDIRECTIONAL NAUSEA BRADYCARDIA RENAL|Digoxin toxicity: hyperkalemia is a marker of severity; give digoxin-specific antibody fragments.
Toxicology|Calcium channel overdose|BLOCKEROVERDOSE|CALCIUM GLUCAGON INSULIN EUGLYCEMIA LIPID PACING VERAPAMIL PROPRANOLOL|High-dose insulin euglycemia therapy improves contractility in beta-blocker and calcium channel blocker overdose.
Toxicology|Lithium trouble|LITHIUMTOXICITY|TREMOR ATAXIA CONFUSION DIALYSIS DEHYDRATION THIAZIDE NSAIDS NEPHROGENIC|Severe lithium toxicity with neurologic symptoms or kidney failure needs hemodialysis.
Toxicology|Stimulants|SYMPATHOMIMETIC|COCAINE AMPHETAMINE TACHYCARDIA HYPERTENSION MYDRIASIS DIAPHORESIS BENZODIAZEPINES HYPERTHERMIA AGITATION SEIZURE ECSTASY|Cocaine chest pain: benzodiazepines first; avoid pure beta-blockers in the acute setting.
Toxicology|Pinpoint pupils|OPIOIDTOXIDROME|MIOSIS BRADYPNEA SEDATION NALOXONE FENTANYL INFUSION NONCARDIOGENIC|Naloxone's duration is shorter than many opioids; watch for renarcotization and consider a drip.
Toxicology|Iron pills|IRONOVERDOSE|DEFEROXAMINE VINROSE ABDOMINAL RADIOPAQUE ACIDOSIS HEPATIC STRICTURE|Iron tablets are radiopaque on X-ray; deferoxamine turns the urine vin-rosé colored.
Toxicology|Ingested caustics|CAUSTIC|ALKALI ACID ENDOSCOPY STRICTURE PERFORATION DRAIN CLEANER BATTERY|Never induce vomiting after caustic ingestion; early endoscopy grades the injury.
Toxicology|Mushroom trouble|POISONINGS|AMANITA MUSHROOM PARAQUAT ARSENIC THALLIUM MERCURY ORGANOPHOSPHATE STRYCHNINE|Amanita phalloides causes delayed GI symptoms followed by acute liver failure 2–3 days later.
Toxicology|Heavy metals|LEADPOISONING|BASOPHILIC STIPPLING FOOTDROP WRISTDROP ENCEPHALOPATHY SUCCIMER DIMERCAPROL|Lead poisoning: microcytic anemia with basophilic stippling, abdominal pain, and neuropathy.
Toxicology|Snake and spider|ENVENOMATION|RATTLESNAKE ANTIVENOM BLACKWIDOW BROWNRECLUSE SCORPION COAGULOPATHY NECROSIS|Pit viper bites cause local swelling and coagulopathy; give antivenom if swelling or coagulopathy progresses.
Pharmacology|Drug interactions|CYPINHIBITORS|CLARITHROMYCIN KETOCONAZOLE GRAPEFRUIT RITONAVIR FLUCONAZOLE AMIODARONE DILTIAZEM VERAPAMIL MACROLIDES AZOLES|CYP3A4 inhibitors raise levels of statins, tacrolimus, and DOACs.
Pharmacology|Speeding up metabolism|CYPINDUCERS|RIFAMPIN CARBAMAZEPINE PHENYTOIN PHENOBARBITAL STJOHNSWORT SMOKING|Rifampin lowers levels of warfarin, DOACs, oral contraceptives, and many HIV drugs.
Pharmacology|QT prolongers|QTPROLONGATION|ONDANSETRON METHADONE HALOPERIDOL AZITHROMYCIN SOTALOL CITALOPRAM DOFETILIDE HYPOKALEMIA TORSADES|Check and replace potassium and magnesium before giving QT-prolonging drugs.
Pharmacology|Pain without opioids|ANALGESIA|ACETAMINOPHEN LIDOCAINE GABAPENTIN DULOXETINE TOPICAL KETAMINE REGIONAL MULTIMODAL|Multimodal analgesia (acetaminophen, regional blocks, gabapentinoids) reduces opioid needs.
Pharmacology|Older adults|BEERSLIST|ANTICHOLINERGICS BENZODIAZEPINES DIPHENHYDRAMINE GLYBURIDE NITROFURANTOIN MUSCLERELAXANTS ZOLPIDEM MEGESTROL AMITRIPTYLINE|Diphenhydramine is on the Beers list: it causes confusion, retention, and falls in older adults.
Pharmacology|Severe skin reactions|STEVENSJOHNSON|ALLOPURINOL LAMOTRIGINE CARBAMAZEPINE SULFONAMIDES NEVIRAPINE MUCOSAL EPIDERMAL SULFA|SJS/TEN is a drug reaction until proven otherwise; stop the culprit and send the patient to a burn unit.
Pharmacology|Drug rash with eosinophils|DRESSRASH|EOSINOPHILIA FACIAL EDEMA HEPATITIS ALLOPURINOL ANTICONVULSANT VANCOMYCIN|DRESS appears 2–8 weeks after the drug starts, with fever, rash, eosinophilia, and organ involvement.
Pharmacology|Steroid-sparing transplant drugs|IMMUNOSUPPRESSION|TACROLIMUS CYCLOSPORINE SIROLIMUS AZATHIOPRINE MYCOPHENOLATE|Azathioprine plus allopurinol can cause severe marrow suppression; check TPMT before starting azathioprine.
Pharmacology|Warfarin details|WARFARIN|VITAMINK INR SKINNECROSIS PROTEINC TERATOGEN BRIDGING DIET INTERACTIONS|Warfarin skin necrosis happens early from protein C depletion; bridge with heparin in high-risk patients.
Pharmacology|Antiplatelets|ANTIPLATELET|ASPIRIN CLOPIDOGREL TICAGRELOR PRASUGREL CILOSTAZOL DIPYRIDAMOLE VORAPAXAR|Clopidogrel is a prodrug activated by CYP2C19; poor metabolizers get less effect.
Pharmacology|Monoclonal names|MONOCLONALS|RITUXIMAB INFLIXIMAB TRASTUZUMAB BEVACIZUMAB PEMBROLIZUMAB DENOSUMAB OMALIZUMAB ADALIMUMAB CETUXIMAB|-ximab is chimeric, -zumab is humanized, and -umab is fully human.
Pharmacology|Statin side effects|MYOPATHY|STATINS MYALGIA CREATINEKINASE RHABDOMYOLYSIS COENZYME GEMFIBROZIL HYPOTHYROIDISM|Statin plus gemfibrozil sharply raises the risk of myopathy; use fenofibrate if a fibrate is needed.
# Dermatology
Dermatology|Itchy rashes|ECZEMA|ATOPIC DERMATITIS FLEXURAL TOPICAL STEROIDS DUPILUMAB EMOLLIENTS LICHENIFICATION|Atopic dermatitis: emollients plus topical steroids; dupilumab for moderate to severe disease.
Dermatology|Silvery plaques|PSORIASIS|PLAQUES EXTENSOR KOEBNER AUSPITZ METHOTREXATE BIOLOGICS NAILPITTING GUTTATE|Guttate psoriasis often follows strep throat; the Koebner phenomenon is new lesions at sites of trauma.
Dermatology|Blisters|BULLOUS|PEMPHIGOID PEMPHIGUS NIKOLSKY DESMOGLEIN ACANTHOLYSIS ELDERLY MUCOSAL TENSE|Bullous pemphigoid: tense blisters in the elderly; pemphigus vulgaris: flaccid blisters, positive Nikolsky sign, and mouth sores.
Dermatology|Skin cancers|SKINCANCER|BASALCELL SQUAMOUS MELANOMA ACTINIC KERATOSIS MOHS SUNSCREEN PEARLY|Basal cell carcinoma is the most common skin cancer; look for a pearly papule with rolled borders and telangiectasias.
Dermatology|Red shins|ERYTHEMANODOSUM|SARCOIDOSIS STREP TUBERCULOSIS PREGNANCY INFLAMMATORYBOWEL PANNICULITIS SHINS|Erythema nodosum: tender red nodules on the shins, often from strep, sarcoid, IBD, or pregnancy.
Dermatology|Target lesions|MULTIFORME|HERPES TARGET MYCOPLASMA ACRAL SIMPLEX ERYTHEMA IRIS ORALULCERS VALACYCLOVIR|Erythema multiforme is most often triggered by herpes simplex and shows target lesions on the hands and feet.
Dermatology|Ulcerating skin|PYODERMA|GANGRENOSUM PATHERGY ULCERATIVE COLITIS RHEUMATOID STEROIDS VIOLACEOUS ULCER|Pyoderma gangrenosum gets worse with debridement (pathergy); treat with steroids, not surgery.
Dermatology|Skin signs of cancer|CUTANEOUSMARKERS|ACANTHOSIS NIGRICANS LESERTRELAT SWEET DERMATOMYOSITIS TRIPE PALMS SIGNS GASTRIC|A sudden eruption of many seborrheic keratoses (Leser-Trélat sign) can signal internal malignancy.
Dermatology|Hair loss|ALOPECIA|AREATA ANDROGENETIC TELOGEN EFFLUVIUM MINOXIDIL FINASTERIDE TRACTION BARICITINIB|Telogen effluvium is diffuse shedding 2–3 months after a stressor such as illness, surgery, or childbirth.
Dermatology|Acne and rosacea|ACNEROSACEA|COMEDONES ISOTRETINOIN DOXYCYCLINE RETINOID FLUSHING METRONIDAZOLE PAPULES SPIRONOLACTONE|Isotretinoin is teratogenic and requires the iPLEDGE program with two forms of contraception.
Dermatology|Fungal skin|DERMATOPHYTES|TINEA CORPORIS CRURIS PEDIS CAPITIS VERSICOLOR TERBINAFINE KOH|Tinea capitis needs oral therapy (terbinafine or griseofulvin); topical agents don't reach the hair shaft.
Dermatology|Itchy bites|SCABIES|BURROWS PERMETHRIN IVERMECTIN WEBSPACES NORWEGIAN CRUSTED MITES PRURITUS|Crusted (Norwegian) scabies in immunocompromised patients is highly contagious; use oral ivermectin plus permethrin.
Dermatology|Hives|URTICARIA|WHEALS HISTAMINE CETIRIZINE OMALIZUMAB CHRONIC ANGIOEDEMA DERMATOGRAPHISM|Chronic urticaria (over 6 weeks) is rarely allergic; treat with up to 4× standard dose second-generation antihistamines.
Dermatology|Purple papules|LICHENPLANUS|PRURITIC PURPLE POLYGONAL PLANAR PAPULES WICKHAM HEPATITIS ORAL|Lichen planus (the six Ps) is associated with hepatitis C; check HCV serology.
Dermatology|Pressure injury|PRESSUREULCER|SACRUM HEEL STAGE REPOSITIONING DEBRIDEMENT NUTRITION MOISTURE BRADEN|Turn immobile patients at least every 2 hours; the Braden scale estimates pressure ulcer risk.
