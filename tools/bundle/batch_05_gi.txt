# GI and hepatology
GI|Heartburn|REFLUX|ESOPHAGITIS BARRETT OMEPRAZOLE FUNDOPLICATION HIATAL HERNIA STRICTURE ADENOCARCINOMA|Barrett esophagus is intestinal metaplasia; it raises the risk of esophageal adenocarcinoma and needs surveillance endoscopy.
GI|Hard to swallow|DYSPHAGIA|ACHALASIA STRICTURE RING WEB EOSINOPHILIC SCLERODERMA ZENKER CANCER SPASM|Dysphagia to solids and liquids from the start suggests a motility disorder such as achalasia; solids only suggests a mechanical blockage.
GI|Bird's beak|ACHALASIA|MANOMETRY MYOTOMY POEM BOTULINUM DILATION CHAGAS PSEUDOACHALASIA BARIUM|Achalasia: absent peristalsis and a tight LES that won't relax. Rule out pseudoachalasia from a GE junction tumor.
GI|Stomach bug|HPYLORI|UREASE BREATHTEST STOOLANTIGEN BISMUTH CLARITHROMYCIN MALT ULCER AMOXICILLIN|Confirm H. pylori eradication with a breath or stool antigen test at least 4 weeks after treatment, off PPIs for 2 weeks.
GI|Ulcer trouble|PEPTICULCER|NSAIDS PYLORI PERFORATION BLEEDING OMEPRAZOLE ZOLLINGER GASTRINOMA ENDOSCOPY|Multiple ulcers beyond the duodenum with diarrhea suggest a gastrinoma (Zollinger-Ellison).
GI|Bowel in trouble|COLITIS|ULCERATIVE ISCHEMIC MICROSCOPIC INFECTIOUS CLOSTRIDIOIDES RADIATION MESALAMINE TOXIC|Ulcerative colitis starts in the rectum and spreads continuously; Crohn disease can skip.
GI|Gluten trouble|CELIAC|TRANSGLUTAMINASE VILLOUS ATROPHY DERMATITIS HERPETIFORMIS GLUTENFREE IRON BIOPSY|Check total IgA with tissue transglutaminase IgA, because IgA deficiency is more common in celiac disease and causes false negatives.
GI|Belly pain and bloating|IRRITABLEBOWEL|ROME CRAMPING BLOATING CONSTIPATION DIARRHEA FODMAP LINACLOTIDE RIFAXIMIN|Alarm features that argue against IBS: age over 50 at onset, bleeding, weight loss, nocturnal symptoms, and iron deficiency.
GI|Colon outpouching|DIVERTICULITIS|SIGMOID FEVER LEFTLOWER ABSCESS PERFORATION FISTULA COLECTOMY FIBER|Uncomplicated diverticulitis in a healthy outpatient often doesn't need antibiotics (DIABOLO, AVOD).
GI|Colon cancer screening|COLONOSCOPY|POLYPS ADENOMA FECAL IMMUNOCHEMICAL STOOLDNA FORTYFIVE LYNCH SIGMOIDOSCOPY|Average-risk colorectal cancer screening starts at age 45.
GI|Hereditary colon cancer|POLYPOSIS|FAMILIAL ADENOMATOUS LYNCH PEUTZJEGHERS GARDNER TURCOT COLECTOMY MICROSATELLITE|Lynch syndrome raises the risk of colon, endometrial, ovarian, and other cancers; tumors show microsatellite instability.
GI|Liver numbers|LIVERTESTS|ALBUMIN BILIRUBIN TRANSAMINASES ALKALINE PHOSPHATASE GGT PROTHROMBIN|An AST:ALT ratio above 2 suggests alcohol-related liver disease.
GI|Viral liver|HEPATITISB|SURFACE ANTIGEN CORE ANTIBODY TENOFOVIR ENTECAVIR REACTIVATION CIRRHOSIS|Before rituximab or chemotherapy, screen for hepatitis B; reactivation can cause fatal liver failure.
GI|Curable virus|HEPATITISC|SOFOSBUVIR VELPATASVIR GLECAPREVIR PIBRENTASVIR GENOTYPE SUSTAINED CRYOGLOBULIN|Hepatitis C is curable in more than 95% of patients with 8–12 weeks of direct-acting antivirals.
GI|Fatty liver|STEATOTIC|METABOLIC OBESITY DIABETES FIBROSCAN ELASTOGRAPHY RESMETIROM SEMAGLUTIDE WEIGHTLOSS|Weight loss of 7–10% improves fatty liver inflammation and fibrosis. Use FIB-4 to triage fibrosis risk.
GI|Drinking liver|ALCOHOLIC|HEPATITIS MADDREY PREDNISOLONE LILLE ABSTINENCE JAUNDICE FEVER LEUKOCYTOSIS|Severe alcohol-related hepatitis (Maddrey score 32 or more): consider prednisolone; stop if the Lille score at day 7 is above 0.45.
GI|Iron overload|HEMOCHROMATOSIS|FERRITIN TRANSFERRIN SATURATION HFE PHLEBOTOMY BRONZE DIABETES ARTHROPATHY|Hereditary hemochromatosis: high transferrin saturation and ferritin; treat with phlebotomy.
GI|Copper overload|WILSON|CERULOPLASMIN KAYSERFLEISCHER PENICILLAMINE TRIENTINE ZINC COPPER HEMOLYSIS TREMOR|Wilson disease: low ceruloplasmin, Kayser-Fleischer rings, and liver or neuropsychiatric disease in a young patient.
GI|Autoimmune liver|AUTOIMMUNE|HEPATITIS PRIMARYBILIARY SCLEROSING URSODIOL ANTIMITOCHONDRIAL SMOOTHMUSCLE AZATHIOPRINE PREDNISONE CHOLANGITIS|Primary biliary cholangitis: antimitochondrial antibodies, high alkaline phosphatase, and ursodiol therapy.
GI|Beaded ducts|SCLEROSING|CHOLANGITIS ULCERATIVE COLITIS BEADING MRCP CHOLANGIOCARCINOMA PANCA STRICTURE|Primary sclerosing cholangitis goes with ulcerative colitis and raises the risk of cholangiocarcinoma and colon cancer.
GI|Stones in the gallbladder|GALLSTONES|CHOLECYSTITIS MURPHY CHOLEDOCHOLITHIASIS CHOLANGITIS ERCP CHOLECYSTECTOMY ULTRASOUND|Charcot triad (fever, jaundice, RUQ pain) means cholangitis and urgent biliary drainage.
GI|Confused with cirrhosis|ENCEPHALOPATHY|LACTULOSE RIFAXIMIN AMMONIA ASTERIXIS INFECTION BLEEDING CONSTIPATION ZINC|Look for a trigger in hepatic encephalopathy: infection, GI bleeding, constipation, electrolytes, or sedatives.
GI|Belly fluid tap|PARACENTESIS|ALBUMIN SAAG NEUTROPHILS CULTURE PROTEIN AMYLASE TRIGLYCERIDES SPONTANEOUS|A SAAG of 1.1 or more means portal hypertension; ascitic neutrophils of 250 or more mean SBP.
GI|Portal pressure|PORTALHYPERTENSION|VARICES SPLENOMEGALY ASCITES CAPUTMEDUSAE TIPS PROPRANOLOL CARVEDILOL BANDING|Nonselective beta-blockers such as carvedilol prevent first variceal bleeds in cirrhosis.
GI|Blood clot in the liver|BUDDCHIARI|HEPATIC VEIN THROMBOSIS POLYCYTHEMIA JAK ASCITES HEPATOMEGALY TIPS|Budd-Chiari syndrome: hepatic vein thrombosis, often from polycythemia vera; test for JAK2.
GI|Liver mass|LIVERTUMOR|HEPATOCELLULAR ADENOMA HEMANGIOMA FOCALNODULAR METASTASIS ALPHAFETOPROTEIN CIRRHOSIS|Patients with cirrhosis need ultrasound with or without AFP every 6 months to screen for hepatocellular carcinoma.
GI|Gut won't move|GASTROPARESIS|DIABETES METOCLOPRAMIDE ERYTHROMYCIN EMPTYING SCAN NAUSEA BLOATING OPIOIDS|A 4-hour gastric emptying scan confirms gastroparesis; stop opioids and GLP-1 agonists that can mimic it.
GI|Blocked bowel|OBSTRUCTION|ADHESIONS HERNIA VOLVULUS INTUSSUSCEPTION CANCER DECOMPRESSION NASOGASTRIC FEVER|Adhesions from prior surgery are the most common cause of small bowel obstruction in adults.
GI|Twisted colon|VOLVULUS|SIGMOID CECAL COFFEEBEAN ELDERLY DECOMPRESSION ENDOSCOPY COLECTOMY|Sigmoid volvulus shows a coffee-bean sign on X-ray and is usually decompressed endoscopically first.
GI|Huge colon|MEGACOLON|TOXIC CLOSTRIDIOIDES ULCERATIVE OGILVIE NEOSTIGMINE DILATION PSEUDOOBSTRUCTION|Ogilvie syndrome (acute colonic pseudo-obstruction) that doesn't resolve can be treated with neostigmine.
GI|Hospital diarrhea|CDIFFICILE|FIDAXOMICIN VANCOMYCIN BEZLOTOXUMAB TOXIN FECAL TRANSPLANT RECURRENCE ANTIBIOTICS|Oral vancomycin or fidaxomicin treats C. difficile. Fecal microbiota transplant is for repeated recurrences.
GI|Pancreas not working|EXOCRINE|STEATORRHEA ELASTASE LIPASE PANCRELIPASE CHRONIC PANCREATITIS CYSTICFIBROSIS DIABETES|Fecal elastase screens for exocrine pancreatic insufficiency; treat with pancreatic enzyme replacement with meals.
GI|Pancreas mass|PANCREATICCANCER|ADENOCARCINOMA COURVOISIER WHIPPLE JAUNDICE TROUSSEAU WEIGHTLOSS GEMCITABINE PAINLESS CELIAC|Painless jaundice with a palpable gallbladder (Courvoisier sign) suggests a pancreatic head cancer.
GI|Gut hormone tumors|NEUROENDOCRINE|CARCINOID INSULINOMA GASTRINOMA VIPOMA GLUCAGONOMA SOMATOSTATINOMA OCTREOTIDE SEROTONIN FLUSHING|Carcinoid syndrome (flushing, diarrhea, wheeze) usually means liver metastases; check urine 5-HIAA.
GI|Blood loss you can't see|OCCULTBLEED|IRON DEFICIENCY CAPSULE ANGIODYSPLASIA COLONOSCOPY ENDOSCOPY ENTEROSCOPY CELIAC|Iron deficiency in a man or postmenopausal woman needs both upper endoscopy and colonoscopy.
GI|Bowel blood supply|SPLANCHNIC|CELIAC SUPERIOR MESENTERIC INFERIOR WATERSHED SPLENICFLEXURE ISCHEMIA|Ischemic colitis favors watershed areas such as the splenic flexure and rectosigmoid junction.
GI|Liver blood tests|CHOLESTASIS|ALKALINE PHOSPHATASE BILIRUBIN PRURITUS URSODIOL OBSTRUCTION DRUGS PREGNANCY|A high alkaline phosphatase with a high GGT points to the liver; a high alkaline phosphatase with a normal GGT points to bone.
GI|Bad breath, bad belly|SMALLBOWEL|BACTERIAL OVERGROWTH BREATHTEST RIFAXIMIN BLINDLOOP BLOATING DIARRHEA|Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth can cause B12 deficiency with high folate, since bacteria use B12 and make folate.
GI|Liver scoring|MELDSCORE|BILIRUBIN CREATININE SODIUM TRANSPLANT ALBUMIN CHILDPUGH ENCEPHALOPATHY|MELD (bilirubin, INR, creatinine, sodium) predicts 3-month mortality and sets liver transplant priority.
