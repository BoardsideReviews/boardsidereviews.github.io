# Cardiology
Cardiology|Rock the boat|MURMURS|STENOSIS REGURGITATION PROLAPSE CRESCENDO HOLOSYSTOLIC DIASTOLIC RUMBLE THRILL OPENINGSNAP|Severe aortic stenosis: a late-peaking crescendo-decrescendo murmur with a soft or absent S2 and a weak, delayed carotid pulse.
Cardiology|Four doors|HEARTVALVES|MITRAL AORTIC TRICUSPID PULMONIC ANNULUS LEAFLET CHORDAE CUSPS COMMISSURE|Rheumatic heart disease most often scars the mitral valve, and mitral stenosis is its classic late result.
Cardiology|Bring it down|LIPIDLOWERING|ATORVASTATIN ROSUVASTATIN EZETIMIBE EVOLOCUMAB ALIROCUMAB INCLISIRAN BEMPEDOIC NIACIN FENOFIBRATE ICOSAPENT|High-intensity statin means atorvastatin 40–80 mg or rosuvastatin 20–40 mg. Add ezetimibe, then a PCSK9 inhibitor, if LDL stays high.
Cardiology|Under pressure|HYPERTENSION|LISINOPRIL LOSARTAN AMLODIPINE DIURETIC MINOXIDIL LABETALOL HYDRALAZINE CLONIDINE CHLORTHALIDONE|First-line agents are a thiazide-type diuretic, an ACE inhibitor or ARB, or a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker. Never combine an ACE inhibitor with an ARB.
Cardiology|Bring it down slowly|EMERGENCY|NICARDIPINE CLEVIDIPINE ESMOLOL LABETALOL NITROPRUSSIDE NITROGLYCERIN FENOLDOPAM PHENTOLAMINE|Hypertensive emergency: lower the MAP by no more than about 25% in the first hour. Aortic dissection is the exception: SBP below 120 within 20 minutes.
Cardiology|Tearing pain|DISSECTION|STANFORD DEBAKEY MARFAN TEARING BICUSPID LOEYSDIETZ ESMOLOL MEDIASTINUM PULSEDEFICIT|Type A (ascending) dissection goes to surgery. Type B is usually managed medically with heart rate below 60 and SBP below 120.
Cardiology|Can't-miss chest pain|CHESTPAIN|ANGINA DISSECTION PERICARDITIS EMBOLISM PNEUMOTHORAX BOERHAAVE TAMPONADE SHINGLES|Six can't-miss causes of chest pain: acute coronary syndrome, aortic dissection, PE, tension pneumothorax, tamponade, and esophageal rupture.
Cardiology|Open the artery|ACUTECORONARY|ASPIRIN HEPARIN TICAGRELOR PRASUGREL CLOPIDOGREL CANGRELOR EPTIFIBATIDE ENOXAPARIN STATIN|Prasugrel is contraindicated after a prior stroke or TIA.
Cardiology|Read the strip|TWELVELEAD|QTINTERVAL PRINTERVAL STSEGMENT DELTAWAVE PWAVE TWAVE UWAVE JPOINT AXIS|U waves suggest hypokalemia, peaked T waves suggest hyperkalemia, and a delta wave with a short PR means pre-excitation (WPW).
Cardiology|Dropped beats|HEARTBLOCKS|FIRSTDEGREE MOBITZ WENCKEBACH COMPLETE PACEMAKER ATROPINE DISSOCIATION ESCAPE|Mobitz II and complete heart block usually need a pacemaker. Mobitz I (Wenckebach) is usually benign.
Cardiology|Fast and narrow|TACHYARRHYTHMIA|ADENOSINE VAGAL REENTRY DILTIAZEM CARDIOVERSION ACCESSORY AVNRT SINUS|Wide-complex tachycardia is ventricular tachycardia until proven otherwise. Avoid AV nodal blockers in pre-excited atrial fibrillation.
Cardiology|Irregularly irregular|ATRIALFIB|CHADSVASC RATECONTROL RHYTHMCONTROL ABLATION APPENDAGE DILTIAZEM METOPROLOL AMIODARONE APIXABAN|Use CHA2DS2-VASc to decide on anticoagulation. In atrial fibrillation with WPW, give procainamide or cardiovert, never AV nodal blockers.
Cardiology|Code called|CODEBLUE|EPINEPHRINE AMIODARONE DEFIBRILLATE COMPRESSIONS LIDOCAINE ASYSTOLE VFIB AIRWAY|Shockable rhythms (VF, pulseless VT) need early defibrillation. Asystole and PEA get epinephrine every 3–5 minutes, not shocks.
Cardiology|Hs and Ts|REVERSIBLE|HYPOXIA TOXINS TENSION HYDROGEN THROMBOSIS TAMPONADE HYPOVOLEMIA HYPOTHERMIA HYPOKALEMIA|In PEA arrest, run through the Hs and Ts. Bedside ultrasound helps find tamponade, tension pneumothorax, and hypovolemia.
Cardiology|Rub a dub|PERICARDITIS|FRICTIONRUB PLEURITIC COLCHICINE DIFFUSE EFFUSION CONSTRICTIVE UREMIC DRESSLER PRDEPRESSION|Acute pericarditis: diffuse ST elevation with PR depression. Colchicine added to an NSAID cuts the recurrence rate.
Cardiology|Born that way|CONGENITAL|TETRALOGY COARCTATION SEPTALDEFECT DUCTUS EISENMENGER EBSTEIN BICUSPID FONTAN TRANSPOSITION|Coarctation clues: arm hypertension, weak femoral pulses, and rib notching. It often comes with a bicuspid aortic valve.
Cardiology|Down they go|SYNCOPE|VASOVAGAL ORTHOSTATIC ARRHYTHMIA STENOSIS SITUATIONAL BRUGADA LONGQT HYPERTROPHIC CAROTIDSINUS|Syncope during exertion, while lying down, or without warning is cardiac until proven otherwise.
Cardiology|Too young to die|SUDDENDEATH|BRUGADA LONGQT HYPERTROPHIC COMMOTIO MYOCARDITIS ANOMALOUS MARFAN CORONARY ARRHYTHMOGENIC|Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy is a leading cause of sudden cardiac death in young athletes.
Cardiology|Walking hurts|CLAUDICATION|CILOSTAZOL ATHEROSCLEROSIS RESTPAIN GANGRENE BYPASS SMOKING EXERCISE ANKLEBRACHIAL STATIN|Cilostazol improves claudication distance but is contraindicated in heart failure.
Cardiology|Swollen calf|DEEPVEIN|WELLS DOPPLER DDIMER APIXABAN PHLEGMASIA THROMBUS COMPRESSION FEMORAL POPLITEAL|Phlegmasia cerulea dolens is a limb-threatening DVT that may need catheter-directed therapy.
Cardiology|Feel the pulse|PULSES|BISFERIENS PARVUS TARDUS ALTERNANS PARADOXUS CORRIGAN WATERHAMMER BOUNDING THREADY|Pulsus parvus et tardus is the weak, late carotid upstroke of severe aortic stenosis.
Cardiology|Bounding everywhere|WIDEPULSE|CORRIGAN QUINCKE DEMUSSET DUROZIEZ TRAUBE MULLER BECKER HILL|Chronic aortic regurgitation creates a wide pulse pressure, which explains its long list of eponymous bounding-pulse signs.
Cardiology|Big heart, weak squeeze|LOWEJECTION|SACUBITRIL VALSARTAN CARVEDILOL BISOPROLOL EPLERENONE DAPAGLIFLOZIN IVABRADINE DIGOXIN HYDRALAZINE|In Black patients with HFrEF on standard therapy, adding hydralazine plus isosorbide dinitrate lowers mortality (A-HeFT).
Cardiology|Stiff and full|PRESERVEDEF|OBESITY HYPERTENSION DIASTOLIC EMPAGLIFLOZIN SPIRONOLACTONE DIURETIC AMYLOID STIFFNESS|In HFpEF, SGLT2 inhibitors reduce heart failure hospitalization (EMPEROR-Preserved, DELIVER).
Cardiology|Thick walls|HYPERTROPHIC|SEPTAL MAVACAMTEN MYECTOMY ALCOHOL ABLATION DEHYDRATION VALSALVA OBSTRUCTION SYSTOLIC|In obstructive HCM the murmur gets louder with Valsalva and standing, and softer with squatting and handgrip.
Cardiology|Squat or strain|MANEUVERS|VALSALVA SQUATTING HANDGRIP STANDING INSPIRATION AMYLNITRITE LEGRAISE PRELOAD AFTERLOAD|Handgrip raises afterload: it makes MR, AR, and VSD murmurs louder and softens the murmurs of HCM and AS.
Cardiology|Electric lifesavers|DEVICES|PACEMAKER DEFIBRILLATOR RESYNCHRONIZATION LEADLESS IMPELLA BALLOONPUMP LVAD ECMO|An ICD is indicated for primary prevention when EF stays at or below 35% despite 3 months of optimal therapy.
Cardiology|Fixing valves|VALVEREPAIR|TAVR TRANSCATHETER MITRACLIP BIOPROSTHETIC MECHANICAL ANNULOPLASTY BALLOON VALVULOPLASTY|Mechanical valves last longer but need lifelong warfarin; bioprosthetic valves avoid warfarin but degenerate over time.
Cardiology|Stress test|ISCHEMIA|TREADMILL DOBUTAMINE ADENOSINE REGADENOSON NUCLEAR ANGIOGRAM PERFUSION ECHOCARDIOGRAM|Patients who can't exercise get pharmacologic stress: dobutamine echo, or vasodilator (adenosine, regadenoson) nuclear perfusion.
Cardiology|Where's the blockage|CORONARIES|LEFTMAIN CIRCUMFLEX DIAGONAL MARGINAL SEPTAL RAMUS RIGHTCORONARY DESCENDING|Inferior ST elevation (II, III, aVF) usually means the right coronary artery. Check right-sided leads for RV infarction.
Cardiology|Inferior and hypotensive|RIGHTVENTRICLE|PRELOAD FLUIDS NITRATES KUSSMAUL HYPOTENSION INFERIOR BRADYCARDIA DOBUTAMINE|RV infarction is preload-dependent: give fluids and avoid nitrates and diuretics.
Cardiology|Heart muscle on fire|MYOCARDITIS|VIRAL TROPONIN GIANTCELL EOSINOPHILIC BIOPSY CHECKPOINT VACCINE SARCOID ARRHYTHMIA|Giant cell myocarditis is rapidly progressive and often fatal without immunosuppression or transplant.
Cardiology|Ballooning apex|TAKOTSUBO|STRESS APICAL BALLOONING CATECHOLAMINE POSTMENOPAUSAL REVERSIBLE TROPONIN EMOTIONAL|Takotsubo mimics a STEMI but has no culprit coronary lesion, and the wall motion usually recovers within weeks.
Cardiology|Pump failure|CARDIOGENIC|LACTATE COLD WET DOBUTAMINE MILRINONE IMPELLA BALLOONPUMP ECMO NOREPINEPHRINE|Cardiogenic shock: cold and wet. Revascularize the culprit artery early when it's caused by an MI.
Cardiology|Cold and wet|HEMODYNAMIC|WARM DRY COLD WET PROFILE PERFUSION CONGESTION INOTROPE DIURESIS|In decompensated heart failure, 'warm and wet' needs diuresis; 'cold and wet' needs perfusion support plus diuresis.
Cardiology|Heart attack labs|BIOMARKERS|TROPONIN CREATINEKINASE MYOGLOBIN NATRIURETIC PEPTIDE HIGHSENSITIVITY DELTA LACTATE|High-sensitivity troponin: the rise and fall (the delta) separates an acute MI from chronic myocardial injury.
Cardiology|Can't lie flat|DYSPNEA|ORTHOPNEA PAROXYSMAL NOCTURNAL TREPOPNEA PLATYPNEA EXERTIONAL WHEEZE CRACKLES|Platypnea-orthodeoxia (worse lying upright) suggests an intracardiac shunt such as a PFO, or hepatopulmonary syndrome.
Cardiology|Rheumatic fever|JONESCRITERIA|CARDITIS POLYARTHRITIS CHOREA ERYTHEMA MARGINATUM NODULES FEVER ARTHRALGIA STREP|Sydenham chorea can be the only sign of acute rheumatic fever, appearing months after the strep infection.
Cardiology|Echo words|ECHOCARDIOGRAM|EJECTION FRACTION STRAIN DOPPLER EFFUSION WALLMOTION GRADIENT SEPTUM TRANSESOPHAGEAL|Transesophageal echo is far more sensitive than transthoracic echo for valve vegetations and left atrial appendage thrombus.
Cardiology|Hypertensive heart|LEFTVENTRICLE|HYPERTROPHY DILATION THROMBUS APEX ANEURYSM CONCENTRIC ECCENTRIC REMODELING|Concentric hypertrophy follows pressure overload (hypertension, AS); eccentric dilation follows volume overload (AR, MR).
Cardiology|Under the knife|PREOPRISK|SURGERY CORONARY HEARTFAILURE STROKE INSULIN CREATININE STAIRS METS FUNCTIONAL|The Revised Cardiac Risk Index counts high-risk surgery, ischemic heart disease, heart failure, cerebrovascular disease, insulin-treated diabetes, and creatinine above 2.
