# Critical care
Critical care|Where's the infection|SEPSISSOURCE|PNEUMONIA URINARY ABDOMINAL CHOLANGITIS CELLULITIS LINES MENINGITIS ENDOCARDITIS EMPYEMA|Source control (drain the abscess, pull the infected line, relieve the obstruction) matters as much as the antibiotic.
Critical care|Lines and tubes|ACCESS|CENTRALLINE ARTERIAL INTRAOSSEOUS PERIPHERAL JUGULAR SUBCLAVIAN FEMORAL ULTRASOUND MIDLINE|Subclavian central lines have the lowest infection rate but the highest pneumothorax risk; use ultrasound for jugular access.
Critical care|Bleeding out|MASSIVETRANSFUSION|PLASMA PLATELETS CALCIUM TRANEXAMIC FIBRINOGEN REDCELLS WARMING|Massive transfusion: give plasma, platelets, and red cells in roughly 1:1:1, and replace calcium as citrate binds it.
Critical care|Very cold|HYPOTHERMIA|OSBORN SHIVERING REWARMING BRADYCARDIA ECMO AFTERDROP IMMERSION ALCOHOL|Hypothermia's J (Osborn) wave sits at the end of the QRS. You're not dead until you're warm and dead.
Critical care|Very hot|HEATSTROKE|EVAPORATIVE ICEWATER IMMERSION RHABDOMYOLYSIS EXERTIONAL CLASSIC DANTROLENE COOLING|Exertional heatstroke: cold-water immersion is the fastest way to cool. Antipyretics don't work because the set point is normal.
Critical care|Rigid and burning up|MALIGNANT|DANTROLENE SUCCINYLCHOLINE SEVOFLURANE RYANODINE HYPERCARBIA RIGIDITY HYPERTHERMIA MASSETER|Malignant hyperthermia: rising end-tidal CO2 is often the first sign. Stop the trigger and give dantrolene.
Critical care|Neuroleptic danger|NEUROLEPTIC|RIGIDITY HYPERTHERMIA CREATINEKINASE HALOPERIDOL BROMOCRIPTINE DANTROLENE AUTONOMIC LEADPIPE|Neuroleptic malignant syndrome develops over days with lead-pipe rigidity; stop the drug and support the patient.
Critical care|Muscle breakdown|RHABDOMYOLYSIS|CREATINEKINASE MYOGLOBIN HYPERKALEMIA FLUIDS STATINS CRUSH COMPARTMENT|Rhabdomyolysis: urine dipstick positive for blood with no red cells on microscopy. Treat with aggressive IV fluids.
Critical care|Tight compartment|COMPARTMENT|FASCIOTOMY PAIN PASSIVE STRETCH PRESSURE PULSELESS PARESTHESIA CRUSH|Pain out of proportion and pain with passive stretch are the earliest signs; a lost pulse is a late sign.
Critical care|Brain swelling|INTRACRANIAL|MANNITOL HYPERTONIC HYPERVENTILATION CUSHING HERNIATION DRAIN ELEVATION SEDATION|Cushing's triad of raised ICP: hypertension, bradycardia, and irregular breathing.
Critical care|No brain function|BRAINDEATH|APNEATEST COMA PUPILS CORNEAL GAG OCULOCEPHALIC CALORIC REFLEXES|Brain death testing requires a known cause, no confounders, absent brainstem reflexes, and a positive apnea test.
Critical care|Liver failing fast|FULMINANT|ACETAMINOPHEN ENCEPHALOPATHY INR CEREBRALEDEMA TRANSPLANT ACETYLCYSTEINE HYPOGLYCEMIA HEPATITIS AMMONIA LACTULOSE|Acute liver failure: give N-acetylcysteine even when the cause isn't acetaminophen; it improves transplant-free survival.
Critical care|Breathing trial|WEANING|SPONTANEOUS BREATHING TRIAL RSBI EXTUBATION CUFFLEAK PRESSURESUPPORT TPIECE|A rapid shallow breathing index (RR divided by tidal volume in liters) below 105 predicts successful extubation.
Critical care|Asynchrony|DYSSYNCHRONY|DOUBLE TRIGGER INEFFECTIVE AUTOTRIGGER FLOWSTARVATION REVERSE CYCLING PREMATURE|Double triggering stacks breaths and raises tidal volumes; it often means the set inspiratory time is too short.
Critical care|Trapped air|AUTOPEEP|EXPIRATORY HOLD COPD ASTHMA RATE TIDALVOLUME DISCONNECT HYPOTENSION BRONCHOSPASM|Auto-PEEP causing hypotension: briefly disconnect the ventilator, then slow the rate and lengthen expiration.
Critical care|Nutrition in the ICU|FEEDING|ENTERAL PARENTERAL REFEEDING PHOSPHATE TUBE TROPHIC PROTEIN CALORIES|Refeeding syndrome's hallmark is hypophosphatemia. Start feeds slowly in malnourished patients and replace electrolytes.
Critical care|Prevent the preventable|ICUBUNDLE|HEADOFBED ULCER PROPHYLAXIS SEDATION VACATION GLUCOSE MOBILITY CATHETERS|Daily spontaneous awakening paired with breathing trials shortens ventilation and ICU stay.
Critical care|Kidneys stopped|CONTINUOUSRENAL|CRRT CITRATE FILTER EFFLUENT ULTRAFILTRATION HEMODIAFILTRATION|Regional citrate anticoagulation for CRRT can cause hypocalcemia and, with liver failure, citrate toxicity.
Critical care|Pregnant in the ICU|OBSTETRIC|ECLAMPSIA MAGNESIUM HELLP PREECLAMPSIA AMNIOTIC EMBOLISM PERIPARTUM HEMORRHAGE|Magnesium sulfate prevents and treats eclamptic seizures; watch for loss of reflexes and respiratory depression.
Critical care|Burn unit|BURNUNIT|PARKLAND RULEOFNINES ESCHAROTOMY INHALATION CARBONMONOXIDE CYANIDE FLUIDS|Parkland formula: 4 mL × kg × % TBSA in the first 24 hours, half of it in the first 8 hours.
Critical care|Smoke and soot|CARBONMONOXIDE|HYPERBARIC OXYGEN CHERRY HEADACHE COOXIMETRY CARBOXYHEMOGLOBIN PULSEOX|Pulse oximetry falsely reads normal in carbon monoxide poisoning; you need co-oximetry.
Critical care|Bitter almonds|CYANIDE|HYDROXOCOBALAMIN NITRITE THIOSULFATE LACTATE SMOKE NITROPRUSSIDE ALMOND MITOCHONDRIA|Cyanide poisoning causes severe lactic acidosis with a high venous oxygen saturation, because cells can't use oxygen.
Critical care|Blue blood|METHEMOGLOBIN|DAPSONE BENZOCAINE NITRITES METHYLENEBLUE CYANOSIS CHOCOLATE GAP|Methemoglobinemia: saturation gap with chocolate-brown blood; treat with methylene blue unless the patient has G6PD deficiency.
Critical care|Severe allergic reaction|ANAPHYLAXIS|EPINEPHRINE INTRAMUSCULAR THIGH ANGIOEDEMA URTICARIA STRIDOR BIPHASIC GLUCAGON TRYPTASE|IM epinephrine in the thigh is first-line; give glucagon if the patient is on a beta-blocker and not responding.
Critical care|Swollen without hives|ANGIOEDEMA|BRADYKININ ACEINHIBITOR ICATIBANT ECALLANTIDE HEREDITARY ESTERASE AIRWAY TONGUE|ACE-inhibitor angioedema is bradykinin-mediated, so epinephrine and antihistamines often don't help. Protect the airway.
Critical care|Life-saving labs|POINTOFCARE|LACTATE GLUCOSE TROPONIN HEMOGLOBIN POTASSIUM IONIZED KETONES|Ionized calcium, not total calcium, guides replacement in critically ill and massively transfused patients.
Critical care|Bedside ultrasound|POCUSEXAM|BLINES LUNGSLIDING EFFUSION TAMPONADE VEXUS IVC APICAL SUBXIPHOID FAST|Absent lung sliding with a lung point confirms pneumothorax at the bedside.
Critical care|Venous congestion|VEXUSGRADE|HEPATIC PORTAL INTRARENAL PULSATILITY IVC DOPPLER CONGESTION DIURESIS|VExUS grades venous congestion from IVC size plus hepatic, portal, and intrarenal vein Doppler patterns.
Critical care|Fluid responsive?|FLUIDRESPONSE|PASSIVELEGRAISE PULSEPRESSURE VARIATION STROKEVOLUME IVC CHALLENGE MINIBOLUS LACTATE REFILL BOLUS CAPILLARY|A passive leg raise is a reversible fluid challenge; a 10% rise in cardiac output predicts fluid responsiveness.
Critical care|Steroids in the unit|STRESSDOSE|HYDROCORTISONE FLUDROCORTISONE ADRENAL SEPTICSHOCK ETOMIDATE CORTISOL|Patients on chronic steroids need stress-dose hydrocortisone during critical illness or surgery.
Critical care|Bleeding on a thinner|REVERSAL|VITAMINK PROTHROMBIN COMPLEX IDARUCIZUMAB ANDEXANET PROTAMINE PLASMA DESMOPRESSIN|Dabigatran is reversed by idarucizumab; apixaban and rivaroxaban by andexanet alfa or PCC; heparin by protamine.
Critical care|Brain bleed on warfarin|WARFARINBLEED|VITAMINK INR PROTHROMBIN FACTORS PLASMA HEMORRHAGE REVERSAL|For warfarin-associated intracranial bleeding, give 4-factor PCC plus IV vitamin K rather than plasma alone.
Critical care|Severe asthma attack|STATUSASTHMATICUS|MAGNESIUM KETAMINE HELIOX ALBUTEROL STEROIDS ECMO|A normal or rising PaCO2 in a severe asthma attack means the patient is tiring and nearing respiratory failure.
Critical care|Paralyzed and ventilated|PARALYTICS|ROCURONIUM CISATRACURIUM VECURONIUM SUGAMMADEX TRAINOFFOUR TWITCH SEDATION NEOSTIGMINE|Cisatracurium is cleared by Hofmann elimination, so it is the paralytic of choice in liver or kidney failure.
Critical care|Lose a limb|LIMBISCHEMIA|PALLOR PULSELESS PARESTHESIA PARALYSIS POIKILOTHERMIA PAIN HEPARIN EMBOLECTOMY|Acute limb ischemia's six Ps: pain, pallor, pulselessness, paresthesia, paralysis, and poikilothermia.
Critical care|Gut dying|MESENTERIC|ISCHEMIA ATRIAL EMBOLUS LACTATE PAINOUT PROPORTION ANGIOGRAPHY LAPAROTOMY|Acute mesenteric ischemia: pain far out of proportion to a soft belly exam, often in a patient with atrial fibrillation.
Critical care|Belly too tight|BELLYPRESSURE|BLADDERPRESSURE OLIGURIA DECOMPRESSION LAPAROTOMY ASCITES DISTENSION PERFUSION|Abdominal compartment syndrome: bladder pressure above 20 mmHg with new organ failure needs decompression.
Critical care|Out of control sugar|HYPEROSMOLAR|GLUCOSE DEHYDRATION OSMOLALITY ELDERLY INSULIN FLUIDS COMA THROMBOSIS|Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state needs more fluid than DKA; glucose is often above 600 with little ketosis.
Critical care|Thyroid gone wild|THYROIDSTORM|PROPRANOLOL PROPYLTHIOURACIL IODINE HYDROCORTISONE FEVER TACHYCARDIA DELIRIUM BURCHWARTOFSKY|Thyroid storm: beta-blocker, thionamide, then iodine an hour later, plus hydrocortisone.
Critical care|Adrenal crash|ADRENALCRISIS|HYDROCORTISONE HYPOTENSION HYPONATREMIA HYPERKALEMIA HYPOGLYCEMIA FLUIDS VOMITING SALINE STEROIDS CORTISOL|Suspected adrenal crisis: give hydrocortisone 100 mg IV right away. Don't wait for a cortisol result.
Critical care|Low and slow|MYXEDEMACOMA|LEVOTHYROXINE HYDROCORTISONE HYPOTHERMIA HYPONATREMIA BRADYCARDIA HYPOVENTILATION EDEMA SEIZURE CONFUSION ILEUS|Myxedema coma: give IV levothyroxine plus stress-dose hydrocortisone until adrenal insufficiency is ruled out.
Critical care|Respiratory failure types|HYPERCAPNIC|COPD OVERDOSE OBESITY NEUROMUSCULAR KYPHOSCOLIOSIS FATIGUE DEADSPACE|Hypercapnic failure is a ventilation problem: low respiratory drive, weak pump, or too much dead space.
Critical care|Blood pressure line|ARTERIALLINE|RADIAL FEMORAL ALLEN WAVEFORM DAMPENING TRANSDUCER ZERO PHLEBOSTATIC|Level the arterial transducer at the phlebostatic axis (4th intercostal space, mid-axillary line).
