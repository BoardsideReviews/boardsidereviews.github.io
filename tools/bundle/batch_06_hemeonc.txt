# Hematology and oncology
Hematology|Clotting cascade|COAGULATION|THROMBIN FIBRINOGEN PROTHROMBIN TISSUEFACTOR ANTITHROMBIN PLASMIN FACTORS PROTEINC VONWILLEBRAND|PT tracks the extrinsic pathway (factor VII) and warfarin; aPTT tracks the intrinsic pathway and unfractionated heparin.
Hematology|Clot-prone|THROMBOPHILIA|FACTORVLEIDEN PROTHROMBIN ANTIPHOSPHOLIPID PROTEINC PROTEINS ANTITHROMBIN HOMOCYSTEINE WARFARIN|Antiphospholipid syndrome needs warfarin, not a DOAC, especially when triple-positive.
Hematology|Low platelets|THROMBOCYTOPENIA|IMMUNE HEPARIN SEPSIS LIVER TTP DIC SPLENOMEGALY ALCOHOL PSEUDO|Always rule out pseudothrombocytopenia (EDTA clumping) by repeating the count in a citrate tube.
Hematology|Heparin gone wrong|HEPARININDUCED|PLATELET FACTOR ARGATROBAN BIVALIRUDIN FONDAPARINUX THROMBOSIS FOURTS SEROTONIN|HIT: platelets drop 50% about 5–10 days after heparin, with clotting, not bleeding. Stop all heparin and start a non-heparin anticoagulant.
Hematology|Clots and bleeding at once|DISSEMINATED|INTRAVASCULAR COAGULATION FIBRINOGEN DDIMER SCHISTOCYTES SEPSIS PROMYELOCYTIC CRYOPRECIPITATE|DIC: low platelets and fibrinogen, high D-dimer, prolonged PT and aPTT. Treat the underlying cause.
Hematology|Pentad of trouble|THROMBOTIC|PURPURA ADAMTS PLASMAPHERESIS CAPLACIZUMAB SCHISTOCYTES FEVER NEUROLOGIC RENAL|TTP: severe ADAMTS13 deficiency. Start plasma exchange urgently; don't wait for the ADAMTS13 result.
Hematology|Kids and E. coli|HEMOLYTICUREMIC|SHIGATOXIN DIARRHEA ECULIZUMAB COMPLEMENT SCHISTOCYTES CHILDREN DIALYSIS ECOLI|Atypical (complement-mediated) HUS is treated with a complement inhibitor such as eculizumab.
Hematology|Bleeding disorders|BLEEDING|HEMOPHILIA VONWILLEBRAND DESMOPRESSIN FACTOREIGHT FACTORNINE MENORRHAGIA EPISTAXIS PLATELETS|Von Willebrand disease is the most common inherited bleeding disorder; desmopressin helps type 1.
Hematology|Sickle crisis|SICKLECELL|HYDROXYUREA VASOOCCLUSIVE ACUTECHEST SPLENIC SEQUESTRATION PRIAPISM STROKE CRIZANLIZUMAB|Acute chest syndrome: new infiltrate with chest pain or hypoxia in sickle cell disease; may need exchange transfusion.
Hematology|Too many red cells|POLYCYTHEMIA|VERA JAKTWO ERYTHROPOIETIN PHLEBOTOMY HYDROXYUREA PRURITUS SMOKING TESTOSTERONE|Polycythemia vera: JAK2 mutation with low EPO. Secondary polycythemia (smoking, OSA, testosterone) has normal-to-high EPO.
Hematology|Too many platelets|THROMBOCYTOSIS|ESSENTIAL REACTIVE IRONDEFICIENCY INFLAMMATION CALRETICULIN ASPIRIN HYDROXYUREA SPLENECTOMY JAKTWO|Most high platelet counts are reactive (iron deficiency, inflammation, splenectomy), not essential thrombocythemia.
Hematology|Marrow scarring|MYELOFIBROSIS|TEARDROP DRYTAP SPLENOMEGALY RUXOLITINIB JAKTWO CALRETICULIN FIBROSIS LEUKOERYTHROBLASTIC|Myelofibrosis: teardrop cells, a dry tap, and a huge spleen; ruxolitinib shrinks the spleen and eases symptoms.
Hematology|Bone marrow cancer|MYELOMA|CALCIUM RENAL ANEMIA BONES MPROTEIN BENCEJONES DARATUMUMAB LENALIDOMIDE BORTEZOMIB|Myeloma CRAB: hypercalcemia, renal failure, anemia, and bone lesions.
Hematology|Smoldering protein|GAMMOPATHY|MONOCLONAL GAMMOPATHY FREELIGHT CHAINS SPEP ELECTROPHORESIS RISK MYELOMA|MGUS progresses to myeloma or a related disorder at about 1% per year.
Hematology|Acute leukemia|LEUKEMIA|MYELOID LYMPHOBLASTIC AUERRODS BLASTS PROMYELOCYTIC TRETINOIN ARSENIC CHRONIC|Acute promyelocytic leukemia: start all-trans retinoic acid immediately when suspected, because DIC kills early.
Hematology|Philadelphia|CHRONICMYELOID|BCRABL IMATINIB DASATINIB NILOTINIB BASOPHILS SPLENOMEGALY BLASTCRISIS|CML is driven by the BCR-ABL fusion (Philadelphia chromosome) and treated with tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as imatinib.
Hematology|Smudge cells|LYMPHOCYTIC|SMUDGE IBRUTINIB VENETOCLAX RICHTER HYPOGAMMAGLOBULINEMIA AUTOIMMUNE ELDERLY LYMPHOCYTOSIS|CLL causes autoimmune hemolytic anemia and hypogammaglobulinemia; Richter transformation is a sudden aggressive turn.
Hematology|Lymph node cancer|LYMPHOMA|HODGKIN REEDSTERNBERG DIFFUSE LARGECELL FOLLICULAR BURKITT MANTLE RITUXIMAB NIGHTSWEATS|B symptoms in lymphoma: fever, drenching night sweats, and weight loss over 10% in 6 months.
Hematology|Marrow failing|APLASTIC|PANCYTOPENIA HYPOCELLULAR ATG CYCLOSPORINE ELTROMBOPAG TRANSPLANT PNH BENZENE|Severe aplastic anemia in a young patient is treated with an HLA-matched sibling transplant when one is available.
Hematology|Dark morning urine|NOCTURNAL|HEMOGLOBINURIA PNH FLOWCYTOMETRY ECULIZUMAB RAVULIZUMAB THROMBOSIS BUDDCHIARI HEMOLYSIS COMPLEMENT ANEMIA|PNH: hemolysis, cytopenias, and clots in odd places such as the hepatic veins. Diagnose by flow cytometry for CD55/CD59.
Hematology|Iron studies|IRONPANEL|FERRITIN TRANSFERRIN SATURATION TIBC HEPCIDIN SERUMIRON INFLAMMATION DEFICIENCY|A ferritin below 30 confirms iron deficiency; ferritin is an acute-phase reactant and can be falsely normal in inflammation.
Hematology|Big red cells|MACROCYTIC|BTWELVE FOLATE ALCOHOL LIVER HYPOTHYROIDISM METHOTREXATE HYDROXYUREA MYELODYSPLASIA RETICULOCYTOSIS|In B12 deficiency, both methylmalonic acid and homocysteine are high; in folate deficiency, only homocysteine is.
Hematology|Antibody attacking red cells|IMMUNEHEMOLYSIS|WARM COLD COOMBS IGG IGM STEROIDS RITUXIMAB MYCOPLASMA|Warm AIHA (IgG) responds to steroids; cold agglutinin disease (IgM) does not, so keep the patient warm.
Hematology|Enzyme shortage|OXIDANTSTRESS|OXIDANT PRIMAQUINE DAPSONE FAVABEANS HEINZ BITECELLS RASBURICASE NITROFURANTOIN|Check G6PD before primaquine, dapsone, or rasburicase; these oxidants can trigger severe hemolysis.
Hematology|Spheres in the smear|SPHEROCYTOSIS|SPECTRIN ANKYRIN OSMOTIC FRAGILITY SPLENECTOMY GALLSTONES MCHC|Hereditary spherocytosis: high MCHC, positive osmotic fragility, and pigment gallstones; splenectomy helps.
Hematology|Neutrophils gone|NEUTROPENICFEVER|CEFEPIME PIPERACILLIN MEROPENEM CHEMOTHERAPY MASCC VANCOMYCIN ANTIFUNGAL CULTURES|Febrile neutropenia: start antipseudomonal antibiotics within an hour. Add vancomycin only for specific indications.
Hematology|Clots in cancer|MALIGNANCYVTE|APIXABAN ENOXAPARIN KHORANA TROUSSEAU PANCREATIC MIGRATORY THROMBOSIS|Cancer-associated VTE: apixaban or rivaroxaban are options, but avoid DOACs in GI cancers with high bleeding risk.
Oncology|Emergency rooms of cancer|ONCEMERGENCY|SPINALCORD COMPRESSION SUPERIORVENACAVA TUMORLYSIS HYPERCALCEMIA LEUKOSTASIS NEUTROPENIA SEIZURE TAMPONADE|Suspected malignant spinal cord compression: give dexamethasone and get an MRI of the whole spine urgently.
Oncology|Blood pressure in the face|SUPERIORVENACAVA|LYMPHOMA SMALLCELL STENT RADIATION SWELLING DYSPNEA|SVC syndrome is rarely an emergency on its own; get a tissue diagnosis before radiating, unless there's airway or brain compromise.
Oncology|Breast cancer|BREASTCANCER|MAMMOGRAM TAMOXIFEN ANASTROZOLE TRASTUZUMAB BRCA LUMPECTOMY ESTROGEN HERTWO|HER2-positive breast cancer is treated with trastuzumab; watch for cardiotoxicity with echo monitoring.
Oncology|Prostate|PROSTATECANCER|PSA GLEASON ENZALUTAMIDE LEUPROLIDE ABIRATERONE BRACHYTHERAPY BONEMETS ACTIVESURVEILLANCE DOCETAXEL FINASTERIDE|Low-risk prostate cancer is often managed with active surveillance rather than immediate treatment.
Oncology|Testicular cancer|TESTICULAR|SEMINOMA NONSEMINOMA ORCHIECTOMY AFP HCG CISPLATIN BLEOMYCIN ETOPOSIDE|Testicular cancer is highly curable even when metastatic, with cisplatin-based chemotherapy.
Oncology|Melanoma ABCDE|MELANOMA|ASYMMETRY BORDER COLOR DIAMETER EVOLVING BRESLOW SENTINEL PEMBROLIZUMAB NIVOLUMAB|Breslow depth (tumor thickness) is the most important prognostic factor in localized melanoma.
Oncology|Head and neck|HEADANDNECK|SQUAMOUS HPV TOBACCO ALCOHOL CETUXIMAB LARYNX OROPHARYNX RADIATION|HPV-positive oropharyngeal cancer has a much better prognosis than tobacco-related cancer.
Oncology|Ovarian|OVARIANCANCER|CA BRCA ASCITES CARBOPLATIN PACLITAXEL OLAPARIB DEBULKING BLOATING|PARP inhibitors such as olaparib work best in BRCA-mutated ovarian cancer.
Oncology|Chemo side effects|CHEMOTOXICITY|CARDIOMYOPATHY NEUROPATHY NEPHROTOXICITY PULMONARYFIBROSIS CYSTITIS MUCOSITIS ALOPECIA OTOTOXICITY|Doxorubicin causes dose-related cardiomyopathy; cyclophosphamide and ifosfamide cause hemorrhagic cystitis (prevent with mesna).
Oncology|Which drug hurts what|CHEMOAGENTS|DOXORUBICIN CISPLATIN BLEOMYCIN VINCRISTINE CYCLOPHOSPHAMIDE METHOTREXATE PACLITAXEL IRINOTECAN ETOPOSIDE OXALIPLATIN|Cisplatin is nephrotoxic and ototoxic; vincristine causes neuropathy; bleomycin causes lung fibrosis.
Oncology|Tumor markers|TUMORMARKERS|ALPHAFETOPROTEIN CALCITONIN CHROMOGRANIN THYROGLOBULIN BETAHCG INHIBIN PARAPROTEIN|Tumor markers are for monitoring known cancers, not screening; CEA tracks colorectal cancer recurrence.
Oncology|Comfort and care|PALLIATIVE|HOSPICE MORPHINE DYSPNEA HALOPERIDOL GLYCOPYRROLATE DEXAMETHASONE SECRETIONS COMFORT|Low-dose opioids relieve dyspnea at the end of life without hastening death when titrated to effect.
Oncology|Performance status|ECOGSCALE|AMBULATORY BEDBOUND SELFCARE KARNOFSKY RESTRICTED ACTIVITY FUNCTIONAL|An ECOG performance status of 3–4 usually means the harms of chemotherapy outweigh the benefits.
Hematology|Stem cell transplant|TRANSPLANTATION|ALLOGENEIC AUTOLOGOUS GRAFTVERSUSHOST CONDITIONING ENGRAFTMENT DONOR CHIMERISM MUCOSITIS RELAPSE|Graft-versus-host disease hits the skin, gut, and liver; the graft-versus-tumor effect is why allogeneic transplants cure.
