# Nephrology
Nephrology|Blood in the urine, red cell casts|NEPHRITIC|HEMATURIA HYPERTENSION OLIGURIA EDEMA POSTSTREP IGANEPHROPATHY LUPUS CRESCENTIC ANTIGBM|Nephritic syndrome: hematuria with red cell casts, hypertension, and a rising creatinine.
Nephrology|Days after a sore throat|POSTSTREP|COMPLEMENT ASLO HEMATURIA EDEMA HYPERTENSION IMPETIGO HUMPS SUBEPITHELIAL|Post-streptococcal GN appears 1–3 weeks after infection with low C3; IgA nephropathy appears within days of a URI with normal complement.
Nephrology|Low complement|HYPOCOMPLEMENTEMIA|LUPUS CRYOGLOBULIN ENDOCARDITIS POSTSTREP MPGN SHUNT|Low-complement glomerulonephritis: lupus, post-infectious, MPGN, cryoglobulinemia, and endocarditis or shunt nephritis.
Nephrology|Lupus kidneys|LUPUSNEPHRITIS|PROLIFERATIVE MEMBRANOUS MYCOPHENOLATE CYCLOPHOSPHAMIDE BELIMUMAB VOCLOSPORIN BIOPSY DSDNA|Class III and IV (proliferative) lupus nephritis needs induction with mycophenolate or cyclophosphamide.
Nephrology|Renal tubular acidosis|TUBULARACIDOSIS|PROXIMAL DISTAL HYPERKALEMIC FANCONI SJOGREN AMPHOTERICIN STONES|Type 1 (distal) RTA: urine pH above 5.5, hypokalemia, and calcium phosphate stones. Type 4 RTA brings hyperkalemia.
Nephrology|Stones|NEPHROLITHIASIS|CALCIUMOXALATE URICACID STRUVITE CYSTINE CITRATE THIAZIDE TAMSULOSIN HYDRATION|Struvite stones form in alkaline urine from urease-producing bacteria such as Proteus and grow into staghorn calculi.
Nephrology|Too much calcium|HYPERCALCEMIA|PARATHYROID MALIGNANCY PTHRP SARCOIDOSIS CALCITONIN ZOLEDRONATE SALINE THIAZIDE MILKALKALI|Severe hypercalcemia: IV saline first, then calcitonin for speed and a bisphosphonate for duration.
Nephrology|Too little calcium|HYPOCALCEMIA|CHVOSTEK TROUSSEAU TETANY MAGNESIUM PARATHYROIDECTOMY PANCREATITIS VITAMIND QTPROLONGED|Check magnesium in hypocalcemia: low magnesium blocks PTH release and action, so calcium won't correct until it's replaced.
Nephrology|Magnesium matters|HYPOMAGNESEMIA|DIURETICS PROTONPUMP ALCOHOL DIARRHEA CISPLATIN TORSADES HYPOKALEMIA AMINOGLYCOSIDE|Hypokalemia won't correct until magnesium is repleted, because low magnesium drives renal potassium wasting.
Nephrology|Phosphate swings|PHOSPHORUS|BINDERS SEVELAMER CALCIUMACETATE LANTHANUM REFEEDING HYPERPARATHYROIDISM CALCIPHYLAXIS|Calciphylaxis: painful necrotic skin lesions in dialysis patients from vascular calcification. Sodium thiosulfate is one treatment.
Nephrology|Kidney bones|RENALBONE|CALCITRIOL CINACALCET PHOSPHATE BINDERS PARATHYROID SEVELAMER CALCIPHYLAXIS FRACTURES|In CKD, low calcitriol and high phosphate drive secondary hyperparathyroidism and renal bone disease.
Nephrology|Slow decline|CHRONICKIDNEY|ALBUMINURIA ANEMIA ACIDOSIS HYPERTENSION FINERENONE EMPAGLIFLOZIN LISINOPRIL ERYTHROPOIETIN|ACE inhibitors or ARBs plus SGLT2 inhibitors slow progression of albuminuric CKD.
Nephrology|Kidney cysts|POLYCYSTIC|AUTOSOMAL TOLVAPTAN ANEURYSM BERRY HEPATIC CYSTS FLANKPAIN HEMATURIA|ADPKD comes with berry aneurysms; tolvaptan slows kidney growth in rapidly progressing disease.
Nephrology|New kidney|KIDNEYTRANSPLANT|TACROLIMUS MYCOPHENOLATE PREDNISONE BELATACEPT REJECTION BKVIRUS CYTOMEGALOVIRUS DONOR|Tacrolimus levels jump with azole antifungals and macrolides, which block CYP3A4.
Nephrology|Kidney and liver|HEPATORENAL|TERLIPRESSIN ALBUMIN MIDODRINE OCTREOTIDE CIRRHOSIS ASCITES NOREPINEPHRINE TRANSPLANT|Hepatorenal syndrome: treat with albumin plus a vasoconstrictor such as terlipressin; liver transplant is the cure.
Nephrology|Contrast and kidneys|CONTRASTNEPHROPATHY|HYDRATION SALINE IODINATED DIABETES METFORMIN VOLUME|Isotonic IV fluids reduce contrast injury risk in high-risk patients; bicarbonate and acetylcysteine add nothing (PRESERVE).
Nephrology|Allergic kidney|DRUGNEPHRITIS|NEPHRITIS PENICILLIN PPI NSAIDS EOSINOPHILURIA RASH FEVER STEROIDS|Acute interstitial nephritis: fever, rash, and eosinophilia classically, but most patients have none of the triad. Stop the drug.
Nephrology|Kidney drug doses|RENALDOSING|ENOXAPARIN METFORMIN GABAPENTIN VANCOMYCIN DABIGATRAN LITHIUM MORPHINE DIGOXIN|Avoid morphine in kidney failure: its active metabolites accumulate and cause sedation and myoclonus.
Nephrology|Fluid choice|CRYSTALLOID|SALINE LACTATED RINGERS BALANCED PLASMALYTE HYPERCHLOREMIC ACIDOSIS ALBUMIN|Balanced crystalloids slightly reduce major kidney events compared with saline in critically ill adults (SMART).
Nephrology|Water excess|SIADHCAUSES|CARBAMAZEPINE SSRI SMALLCELL PNEUMONIA MENINGITIS NAUSEA PAIN SURGERY CYCLOPHOSPHAMIDE|SIADH: euvolemic hyponatremia with urine osmolality above 100 and urine sodium above 30.
Nephrology|Water loss|INSIPIDUS|DESMOPRESSIN LITHIUM HYPERNATREMIA POLYURIA NEPHROGENIC CENTRAL THIAZIDE|Lithium is the most common cause of nephrogenic diabetes insipidus; amiloride can help.
Nephrology|Too much sodium|HYPERNATREMIA|FREEWATER DEFICIT DEHYDRATION ELDERLY THIRST DIABETES INSIPIDUS|Hypernatremia almost always means a lack of access to water, usually in elders or intubated patients.
Nephrology|Swollen kidneys|HYDRONEPHROSIS|OBSTRUCTION STONE PROSTATE NEPHROSTOMY STENT RETROPERITONEAL FIBROSIS ULTRASOUND|An obstructed infected kidney is a urologic emergency: decompress with a stent or nephrostomy.
Nephrology|Clots in the kidney|RENALVEIN|MEMBRANOUS NEPHROTIC FLANKPAIN HEMATURIA ANTICOAGULATION THROMBOSIS PROTEINURIA|Membranous nephropathy carries the highest risk of renal vein thrombosis among nephrotic syndromes.
Nephrology|Kidney artery narrowing|RENOVASCULAR|STENOSIS FIBROMUSCULAR DYSPLASIA ATHEROSCLEROTIC BRUIT FLASH EDEMA ACEINHIBITOR|Fibromuscular dysplasia causes renovascular hypertension in young women and responds well to angioplasty.
Nephrology|Cancer in the kidney|RENALCELL|HEMATURIA FLANKPAIN MASS POLYCYTHEMIA HYPERCALCEMIA VONHIPPELLINDAU SUNITINIB NEPHRECTOMY|The classic renal cell carcinoma triad (hematuria, flank pain, mass) shows up in under 10% of patients.
Nephrology|Myeloma and the kidney|CASTNEPHROPATHY|LIGHTCHAINS AMYLOID BENCEJONES HYPERCALCEMIA MYELOMA DIALYSIS PROTEIN|A urine dipstick misses Bence Jones light chains, so a high urine protein-to-creatinine ratio with a negative dipstick suggests myeloma.
Nephrology|Dialysis access|FISTULA|ARTERIOVENOUS GRAFT CATHETER THRILL BRUIT STEAL STENOSIS PERITONEAL|An AV fistula is the preferred dialysis access: lowest infection and clotting rates. Feel for the thrill.
Nephrology|Peritoneal dialysis trouble|PERITONEAL|PERITONITIS CLOUDY EFFLUENT VANCOMYCIN CEFTAZIDIME INTRAPERITONEAL CATHETER HERNIA|PD peritonitis presents with cloudy effluent and abdominal pain; give intraperitoneal antibiotics.
Nephrology|Urine clues|URINALYSIS|NITRITE ESTERASE CASTS CRYSTALS PROTEIN GLUCOSE BLOOD SPECIFIC GRAVITY|Muddy brown granular casts mean ATN, red cell casts mean glomerulonephritis, and white cell casts mean pyelonephritis or interstitial nephritis.
Nephrology|Toxic alcohols|OSMOLARGAP|METHANOL ETHYLENE GLYCOL ISOPROPANOL FOMEPIZOLE DIALYSIS OXALATE VISION|Isopropanol raises the osmolar gap without causing an anion gap acidosis; it gives ketosis without acidosis.
Nephrology|Urine pH tricks|URINEANIONGAP|DIARRHEA AMMONIUM RTA NEGATIVE POSITIVE CHLORIDE SODIUM POTASSIUM BICARBONATE|In a non-anion gap acidosis, a negative urine anion gap (NH4 excreted) points to diarrhea; a positive one points to RTA.
