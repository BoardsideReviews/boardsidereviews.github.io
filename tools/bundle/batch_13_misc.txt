# Physical exam, allergy, eyes, ENT, stats, ethics, urology, surgery
Physical exam|Eponymous signs|BEDSIDESIGNS|MURPHY KERNIG BABINSKI CHVOSTEK TROUSSEAU HOMANS TINEL PHALEN KUSSMAUL|Kussmaul sign (JVP rises with inspiration) suggests constrictive pericarditis or restrictive cardiomyopathy.
Physical exam|Nail clues|FINGERNAILS|CLUBBING SPLINTER KOILONYCHIA PITTING ONYCHOLYSIS TERRYS BEAU MEES CYANOSIS|Koilonychia (spoon nails) suggests iron deficiency; Mees lines suggest arsenic poisoning.
Physical exam|Clubbed fingers|CLUBBING|LUNGCANCER BRONCHIECTASIS CYSTICFIBROSIS FIBROSIS CYANOTIC ENDOCARDITIS CIRRHOSIS|Clubbing is not a feature of uncomplicated COPD; new clubbing in a smoker should prompt a search for lung cancer.
Physical exam|Neurologic reflexes|REFLEXES|BICEPS TRICEPS PATELLAR ACHILLES BABINSKI HOFFMANN CLONUS PLANTAR GLABELLAR|A positive Babinski or Hoffmann sign means an upper motor neuron lesion.
Physical exam|Jugular veins|JUGULARVENOUS|PRESSURE KUSSMAUL CANNON AWAVE VWAVE HEPATOJUGULAR DISTENSION|Giant V waves in the jugular pulse suggest tricuspid regurgitation; cannon A waves suggest AV dissociation.
Physical exam|Gait types|GAITTYPES|ATAXIC SHUFFLING STEPPAGE WADDLING SPASTIC ANTALGIC MAGNETIC CIRCUMDUCTION|A magnetic gait (feet stuck to the floor) suggests normal pressure hydrocephalus.
Physical exam|Skin signs of liver disease|STIGMATA|SPIDERANGIOMA PALMARERYTHEMA GYNECOMASTIA ASTERIXIS JAUNDICE CAPUTMEDUSAE DUPUYTREN|Spider angiomas and palmar erythema come from excess estrogen in cirrhosis.
Physical exam|Lymph node map|LYMPHNODES|CERVICAL AXILLARY INGUINAL SUPRACLAVICULAR EPITROCHLEAR PREAURICULAR HILAR SISTERMARY|A Sister Mary Joseph nodule (umbilical) signals metastatic intra-abdominal cancer.
Allergy|Penicillin allergy|ALLERGYTESTING|SKINTEST CHALLENGE DESENSITIZATION IGE CROSSREACTIVITY AMOXICILLIN DELABELING|Most people labeled penicillin-allergic aren't; delabeling with testing or a direct oral challenge improves antibiotic choices.
Allergy|Food allergy|FOODALLERGY|PEANUT TREENUT SHELLFISH MILK EGGS SESAME ANAPHYLAXIS EPINEPHRINE|Alpha-gal syndrome (red meat allergy) follows lone star tick bites and causes delayed anaphylaxis.
Allergy|Immune deficiency|IMMUNEDEFECTS|COMMONVARIABLE IGADEFICIENCY BRUTON CHRONICGRANULOMATOUS SEVERECOMBINED DIGEORGE WISKOTTALDRICH|IgA deficiency is the most common primary immunodeficiency; give IgA-depleted blood products to avoid anaphylaxis.
Allergy|Eosinophils everywhere|EOSINOPHILIA|PARASITES ALLERGY DRUGS ASTHMA EGPA LEUKEMIA ADRENAL HYPEREOSINOPHILIC|Eosinophilia causes (NAACP): neoplasm, allergy/asthma, Addison disease, collagen vascular disease, parasites.
Allergy|Mast cells|MASTOCYTOSIS|TRYPTASE URTICARIA PIGMENTOSA DARIER FLUSHING ANAPHYLAXIS KIT HISTAMINE|Systemic mastocytosis: high baseline tryptase, flushing, and anaphylaxis; often a KIT D816V mutation.
Ophthalmology|Eye emergencies|EYEEMERGENCY|ANGLECLOSURE DETACHMENT ARTERYOCCLUSION CHEMICAL ORBITAL CELLULITIS ENDOPHTHALMITIS HYPHEMA GLOBE|Chemical eye injury: irrigate immediately with large volumes, before any other evaluation.
Ophthalmology|Painful red eye|GLAUCOMA|ANGLECLOSURE OPENANGLE INTRAOCULAR PRESSURE TIMOLOL PILOCARPINE ACETAZOLAMIDE HALO|Acute angle-closure glaucoma: painful red eye, halos, and a mid-dilated fixed pupil; avoid anticholinergics.
Ophthalmology|Diabetic eyes|RETINOPATHY|MICROANEURYSMS HEMORRHAGES EXUDATES NEOVASCULARIZATION MACULAREDEMA LASER ANTIVEGF|Diabetic macular edema is treated with intravitreal anti-VEGF injections.
Ophthalmology|Floaters and flashes|RETINALDETACHMENT|FLOATERS FLASHES CURTAIN MYOPIA LASER VITRECTOMY|New flashes and floaters with a curtain over vision: same-day retina evaluation for detachment.
ENT|Nosebleed|EPISTAXIS|ANTERIOR POSTERIOR KIESSELBACH PRESSURE PACKING CAUTERY OXYMETAZOLINE TRANEXAMIC|Most nosebleeds come from Kiesselbach's plexus in the anterior septum; hold firm pressure for 10–15 minutes.
ENT|Hearing loss|HEARINGLOSS|CONDUCTIVE SENSORINEURAL RINNE WEBER PRESBYCUSIS OTOTOXIC STEROIDS ACOUSTIC|Sudden sensorineural hearing loss is an emergency: start oral steroids promptly and get an audiogram.
ENT|Neck mass|NECKMASS|BRANCHIAL THYROGLOSSAL LYMPHADENOPATHY CARCINOMA LIPOMA ABSCESS PAROTID LYMPHOMA|A persistent neck mass in an adult smoker is cancer until proven otherwise; get imaging and an ENT exam.
ENT|Deep neck infections|DEEPSPACE|PERITONSILLAR RETROPHARYNGEAL LUDWIG EPIGLOTTITIS TRISMUS DROOLING AIRWAY|Ludwig angina is a floor-of-mouth infection that can close the airway; secure the airway early.
Statistics|Test performance|DIAGNOSTICS|SENSITIVITY SPECIFICITY PREDICTIVE LIKELIHOOD RATIO PRETEST POSTTEST PREVALENCE|SnNOut and SpPIn: a very sensitive negative test rules out, and a very specific positive test rules in.
Statistics|Trial numbers|BIOSTATISTICS|RELATIVERISK ODDSRATIO HAZARD NUMBERNEEDED CONFIDENCE INTERVAL PVALUE POWER|Number needed to treat = 1 divided by the absolute risk reduction.
Statistics|Study designs|STUDYDESIGN|RANDOMIZED COHORT CASECONTROL CROSSSECTIONAL METAANALYSIS BLINDING INTENTION|Case-control studies are best for rare diseases and report odds ratios.
Statistics|Bias types|STUDYBIAS|SELECTION RECALL LEADTIME LENGTHTIME CONFOUNDING PUBLICATION ATTRITION OBSERVER|Lead-time bias makes screening look like it prolongs survival just by diagnosing disease earlier.
Ethics|Big four|BIOETHICS|AUTONOMY BENEFICENCE NONMALEFICENCE JUSTICE CONFIDENTIALITY CONSENT|Autonomy lets a patient with capacity refuse any treatment, even life-saving treatment.
Ethics|Informed consent|CONSENT|CAPACITY RISKS BENEFITS ALTERNATIVES VOLUNTARY DISCLOSURE INTERPRETER EMERGENCY|Use a professional medical interpreter, not family members, for consent conversations.
Ethics|End of life|ENDOFLIFE|HOSPICE DNR COMFORT DOUBLEEFFECT WITHDRAWAL SURROGATE PALLIATIVE ADVANCE|Withholding and withdrawing life support are ethically equivalent.
Quality|Patient safety|SAFETY|HANDOFF CHECKLIST ROOTCAUSE NEARMISS DISCLOSURE TIMEOUT SWISSCHEESE ERRORS|Disclose medical errors to patients promptly and honestly; it is both ethical and associated with fewer lawsuits.
Urology|Testicular emergency|SCROTALPAIN|TORSION EPIDIDYMITIS ORCHITIS CREMASTERIC PREHN FOURNIER HYDROCELE VARICOCELE|Testicular torsion: absent cremasteric reflex; detorsion within 6 hours saves the testis.
Urology|Blood in urine|HEMATURIA|CYSTOSCOPY UROTHELIAL SMOKING STONES GLOMERULAR CTUROGRAM BLADDERCANCER|Gross hematuria in a smoker needs cystoscopy and CT urography to rule out urothelial cancer.
Surgery|Hernias|HERNIAS|INGUINAL FEMORAL UMBILICAL INCISIONAL HIATAL INCARCERATED STRANGULATED DIRECT INDIRECT|Femoral hernias are more common in women and have the highest strangulation risk.
Surgery|Postop fever|POSTOPFEVER|ATELECTASIS PNEUMONIA URINARY WOUND THROMBOSIS DRUGS ABSCESS LEAK|Postoperative fever timing (wind, water, walking, wound, wonder drugs) points to the likely source.
Surgery|Surgical abdomen|ACUTEABDOMEN|PERITONITIS GUARDING REBOUND RIGIDITY FREEAIR PERFORATION APPENDICITIS ISCHEMIA|Free air under the diaphragm on an upright film means a perforated viscus until proven otherwise.
Surgery|Perioperative meds|PERIOPERATIVE|ANTICOAGULATION BRIDGING METFORMIN SGLTTWO INSULIN STEROIDS BETABLOCKER|Hold SGLT2 inhibitors 3–4 days before surgery to avoid euglycemic DKA.
Surgery|Trauma basics|TRAUMA|AIRWAY BREATHING CIRCULATION DISABILITY EXPOSURE FAST TOURNIQUET HEMORRHAGE|In trauma, stop catastrophic bleeding first; permissive hypotension limits blood loss until surgical control.
Surgery|Head injury|HEADINJURY|EPIDURAL SUBDURAL CONCUSSION LUCID INTERVAL CANADIAN GLASGOW ANTICOAGULANT|Epidural hematoma: a lucid interval, then rapid decline; lens-shaped bleed on CT.
Surgery|Spleen gone|ASPLENIA|PNEUMOCOCCUS MENINGOCOCCUS HAEMOPHILUS HOWELLJOLLY VACCINES SEPSIS CAPSULATED|After splenectomy, fever is an emergency: overwhelming sepsis from encapsulated bacteria can kill within hours.
