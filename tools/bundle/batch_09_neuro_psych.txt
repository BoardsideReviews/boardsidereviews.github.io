# Neurology
Neurology|Weakness that climbs|GUILLAINBARRE|ASCENDING AREFLEXIA CAMPYLOBACTER ALBUMINOCYTOLOGIC IVIG PLASMAPHERESIS VITALCAPACITY|Guillain-Barré: ascending weakness with lost reflexes; CSF shows high protein with a normal cell count. Steroids don't help.
Neurology|Gets worse with use|MYASTHENIA|PTOSIS DIPLOPIA FATIGABLE ACETYLCHOLINE PYRIDOSTIGMINE THYMOMA IVIG ECULIZUMAB|In myasthenic crisis, avoid aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones, magnesium, and beta-blockers.
Neurology|Gets better with use|LAMBERTEATON|CALCIUM CHANNEL SMALLCELL PROXIMAL AUTONOMIC FACILITATION AMIFAMPRIDINE|Lambert-Eaton weakness improves with repeated effort and is associated with small cell lung cancer.
Neurology|Plaques in the brain|MULTIPLESCLEROSIS|OPTICNEURITIS LHERMITTE UHTHOFF OLIGOCLONAL OCRELIZUMAB NATALIZUMAB DEMYELINATION FATIGUE STEROIDS|Natalizumab carries a risk of PML (JC virus); check JC virus antibodies before and during therapy.
Neurology|Motor neuron disease|ALSDISEASE|FASCICULATIONS RILUZOLE EDARAVONE UPPER LOWER BULBAR SPASTICITY HYPERREFLEXIA|ALS mixes upper and lower motor neuron signs with sensation and eye movements spared.
Neurology|Headache types|HEADACHE|MIGRAINE CLUSTER TENSION AURA PHOTOPHOBIA TRIPTAN OXYGEN THUNDERCLAP|Cluster headache: short, severe, unilateral orbital pain with tearing; treat attacks with high-flow oxygen.
Neurology|Worst headache of my life|SUBARACHNOID|THUNDERCLAP ANEURYSM XANTHOCHROMIA NIMODIPINE VASOSPASM COILING CLIPPING|Nimodipine improves outcomes after aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage; vasospasm peaks days 4–14.
Neurology|Bleed in the brain|HEMORRHAGIC|HYPERTENSIVE AMYLOID PUTAMEN THALAMUS CEREBELLAR LOBAR REVERSAL BLOODPRESSURE|Cerebellar hemorrhage larger than 3 cm or with brainstem compression needs urgent surgical evacuation.
Neurology|Clot busters|THROMBOLYSIS|ALTEPLASE TENECTEPLASE LASTKNOWNWELL NIHSS BLOODPRESSURE THROMBECTOMY PERFUSION|Lower blood pressure below 185/110 before giving IV thrombolysis for ischemic stroke.
Neurology|Mini stroke|TRANSIENT|ISCHEMIC ATTACK ABCD CAROTID ECHOCARDIOGRAM CLOPIDOGREL ASPIRIN HOLTER|After a minor stroke or high-risk TIA, 21 days of aspirin plus clopidogrel lowers early recurrence (POINT, CHANCE).
Neurology|Neck artery narrowing|CAROTID|ENDARTERECTOMY STENTING BRUIT STENOSIS AMAUROSIS FUGAX SYMPTOMATIC STATIN|Symptomatic carotid stenosis of 70% or more benefits from endarterectomy, ideally within 2 weeks.
Neurology|Face drooping|BELLSPALSY|FACIALNERVE PREDNISONE FOREHEAD HYPERACUSIS LYME RAMSAYHUNT EYEPATCH|Bell's palsy involves the forehead; a stroke spares it. Steroids within 72 hours improve recovery.
Neurology|Numb feet|NEUROPATHY|DIABETIC ALCOHOL BTWELVE CHEMOTHERAPY GUILLAINBARRE VASCULITIC AMYLOID STOCKING|Diabetes is the most common cause of peripheral neuropathy; check B12, especially in patients on metformin.
Neurology|Spinal cord problems|MYELOPATHY|COMPRESSION TRANSVERSE BTWELVE COPPER SUBACUTE COMBINED LEVEL CERVICAL|Subacute combined degeneration (B12 or copper deficiency) hits the dorsal columns and corticospinal tracts.
Neurology|Spinal cord syndromes|CORDSYNDROMES|ANTERIOR CENTRAL BROWNSEQUARD POSTERIOR CAUDAEQUINA CONUS SYRINX|Central cord syndrome (often from hyperextension) weakens the arms more than the legs.
Neurology|Saddle numbness|CAUDAEQUINA|SADDLE ANESTHESIA RETENTION INCONTINENCE DISCHERNIATION MRI DECOMPRESSION SCIATICA|Cauda equina syndrome is a surgical emergency: get an MRI and a spine surgeon now.
Neurology|Seizure types|EPILEPSY|FOCAL GENERALIZED ABSENCE TONICCLONIC MYOCLONIC ATONIC AURA POSTICTAL|Absence seizures respond to ethosuximide or valproate; carbamazepine can make them worse.
Neurology|Spinal tap|LUMBARPUNCTURE|OPENING PRESSURE PROTEIN GLUCOSE CELLCOUNT XANTHOCHROMIA OLIGOCLONAL CULTURE|Bacterial meningitis CSF: high neutrophils, high protein, and low glucose.
Neurology|Head pressure, no tumor|PSEUDOTUMOR|IDIOPATHIC INTRACRANIAL PAPILLEDEMA ACETAZOLAMIDE OBESITY VISION TOPIRAMATE|Idiopathic intracranial hypertension: young obese woman, headache, papilledema; vision loss is the danger.
Neurology|Dizzy spells|VERTIGO|BPPV EPLEY DIXHALLPIKE VESTIBULAR NEURITIS MENIERE HINTS NYSTAGMUS|HINTS exam in acute vertigo: a normal head impulse test suggests a central cause such as stroke.
Neurology|Tremor types|TREMORS|ESSENTIAL PARKINSONIAN INTENTION PHYSIOLOGIC PROPRANOLOL PRIMIDONE CEREBELLAR RESTING|Essential tremor is postural and action tremor that often improves with alcohol; treat with propranolol or primidone.
Neurology|Moving too much|MOVEMENTDISORDER|CHOREA DYSTONIA TICS ATHETOSIS HEMIBALLISMUS MYOCLONUS TARDIVE AKATHISIA|Hemiballismus usually comes from a lesion in the contralateral subthalamic nucleus.
Neurology|Twisting and jerking|HUNTINGTONS|CHOREA CAG REPEATS CAUDATE TETRABENAZINE DEPRESSION DEMENTIA AUTOSOMAL|Huntington disease: autosomal dominant CAG repeat expansion with caudate atrophy and anticipation.
Neurology|Mad cow|PRIONDISEASE|CREUTZFELDTJAKOB MYOCLONUS PERIODIC SHARPWAVES RAPID DEMENTIA RTQUIC|Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease: rapidly progressive dementia with myoclonus; RT-QuIC on CSF is highly specific.
Neurology|Water on the brain|HYDROCEPHALUS|NORMALPRESSURE GAIT INCONTINENCE DEMENTIA SHUNT TAPTEST VENTRICLES|Normal pressure hydrocephalus: wet, wacky, and wobbly. A large-volume tap test predicts shunt response.
Neurology|Brain tumors|NEUROONCOLOGY|GLIOBLASTOMA MENINGIOMA METASTASIS SCHWANNOMA OLIGODENDROGLIOMA TEMOZOLOMIDE LYMPHOMA SEIZURES EDEMA|Brain metastases are more common than primary brain tumors; lung cancer and melanoma are frequent sources.
Neurology|Brain gaze|EYEMOVEMENTS|NYSTAGMUS INTERNUCLEAR OPHTHALMOPLEGIA CONJUGATE GAZE PALSY SACCADES SKEW|Bilateral internuclear ophthalmoplegia in a young adult strongly suggests multiple sclerosis.
Neurology|Visual field cuts|VISUALFIELDS|HEMIANOPIA QUADRANTANOPIA BITEMPORAL CHIASM PITUITARY OPTICTRACT SCOTOMA|Bitemporal hemianopia means a lesion at the optic chiasm, classically a pituitary adenoma.
Neurology|Coma causes|ALTEREDMENTAL|HYPOGLYCEMIA SEIZURE STROKE UREMIA HEPATIC SEPSIS TOXINS HYPERCAPNIA WERNICKE|In any altered patient, check a fingerstick glucose first and give thiamine before glucose when at risk.
Neurology|Locked in|BRAINSTEM|PONS MEDULLA MIDBRAIN WALLENBERG LOCKEDIN WEBER CROSSED BASILAR|Lateral medullary (Wallenberg) syndrome: crossed facial and body pain-temperature loss, Horner syndrome, and dysphagia.
Neurology|Nerve conduction|ELECTROMYOGRAPHY|DENERVATION FIBRILLATIONS CONDUCTION VELOCITY DEMYELINATING AXONAL REPETITIVE AMPLITUDE LATENCY|Demyelinating neuropathy slows conduction velocity; axonal neuropathy lowers amplitudes.
Neurology|Restless nights|RESTLESSLEGS|FERRITIN IRON PRAMIPEXOLE GABAPENTIN AUGMENTATION URGE EVENING|Restless legs: check ferritin and replace iron if below 75. Dopamine agonists can cause augmentation over time.
# Psychiatry
Psychiatry|Mood stabilizers|BIPOLAR|LITHIUM VALPROATE LAMOTRIGINE CARBAMAZEPINE QUETIAPINE MANIA DEPRESSION HYPOMANIA|Lithium toxicity rises with dehydration, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, and thiazides; it causes tremor, confusion, and diabetes insipidus.
Psychiatry|Low mood|DEPRESSION|ANHEDONIA SSRI SLEEP APPETITE GUILT ENERGY CONCENTRATION PSYCHOMOTOR SUICIDALITY|Depression screening: ask about anhedonia and low mood (PHQ-2); always ask directly about suicidal thoughts.
Psychiatry|Anxious|ANXIETY|PANIC GENERALIZED SERTRALINE BUSPIRONE CBT HYDROXYZINE PHOBIA BENZODIAZEPINE|SSRIs and CBT are first-line for anxiety disorders; benzodiazepines are for short-term use only.
Psychiatry|Alcohol withdrawal|WITHDRAWAL|TREMOR SEIZURES HALLUCINOSIS DELIRIUMTREMENS CIWA LORAZEPAM PHENOBARBITAL THIAMINE|Delirium tremens peaks at 48–96 hours after the last drink and can be fatal; treat with symptom-triggered benzodiazepines.
Psychiatry|Opioid use disorder|ADDICTION|BUPRENORPHINE METHADONE NALTREXONE NALOXONE HARMREDUCTION FENTANYL PRECIPITATED|Buprenorphine and methadone cut overdose deaths; start buprenorphine when the patient is in moderate withdrawal.
Psychiatry|Too little food|EATINGDISORDER|ANOREXIA BULIMIA REFEEDING BRADYCARDIA HYPOKALEMIA AMENORRHEA LANUGO RUSSELL|Refeeding syndrome in anorexia: watch phosphate, potassium, and magnesium closely as feeding restarts.
Psychiatry|Psychosis|SCHIZOPHRENIA|HALLUCINATIONS DELUSIONS NEGATIVE SYMPTOMS CLOZAPINE RISPERIDONE CATATONIA|Catatonia responds to lorazepam; antipsychotics can make it worse or trigger NMS.
Psychiatry|Sleeplessness|INSOMNIA|CBTI HYGIENE MELATONIN TRAZODONE ZOLPIDEM SUVOREXANT RESTRICTION|Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is first-line, ahead of sleep medications.
Psychiatry|Capacity|DECISIONMAKING|CAPACITY UNDERSTAND APPRECIATE REASON CHOICE SURROGATE CONSENT|Capacity means the patient can understand, appreciate, reason, and express a choice; any physician can assess it.
